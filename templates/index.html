Open a room

Start in General, or add a room of your own. Public rooms are open to anyone; private rooms use a shared secret key.

Bring someone along

Exchange contact keys and add each other. Direct messages encrypt the content for the recipient. They need to be online to receive it.

Keep your identity

Your browser creates your keys. Download an identity backup to return as the same person. Keep it private: it contains your secret keys.

Leave a lighter trail

The relay has no message database. Your open tab holds recent messages, and recipients can keep copies. There is no offline inbox.

Privacy deserves plain language.

Encryption protects message content in private conversations. It does not make your connection invisible. Use this experiment with its limits in mind.

What the network can see

Relays and network observers can see addresses, timing, sizes and traffic patterns. Direct peers may learn your address. Public rooms are readable; display names do not verify a person. Pseudonyms, padding and signatures do not establish anonymity. Recorded direct messages may be exposed if the recipient’s long-term key is compromised.

What works today—and what’s next

The experimental client supports public text, shared-key rooms and encrypted direct messages through a WebSocket relay. Direct peer links remain experimental: local Safari tests passed, Chrome direct connectivity is unresolved, and cross-device/WAN delivery is not yet demonstrated. Relay delivery stays available. No STUN/TURN service is configured. A resilient anonymous ad hoc mesh, optional caching, images and video are future work.

Subscriptions, rewards and Hermes credits

These are research plans, not available services. Dummy-credit contracts explore ordinary and advertising credits, double-spend rejection and monthly publisher subscriptions. There are no live purchases, wallets, advertising rewards or blockchain settlements. Watching a video does not prove human attention. The monetary model requires accounting state even when transport is transient.

Identity and retention

Identity backups contain unencrypted secret keys. Import them only locally in the client. Closing a tab can lose an identity without a saved backup. Relays retain bounded temporary connection and duplicate-suppression state; recipients, browsers and infrastructure can retain data independently. Ephemeral does not mean impossible to save.
