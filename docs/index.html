What you can try today

Public text channels, shared-key private channels, and encrypted direct messages in an experimental browser client.

Bring your own keys

The browser generates an identity. Save its configuration to keep your keys and contacts. Treat that unencrypted file as a secret.

Choose the conversation

Public channels are readable by others. Private channels use a shared key. Direct messages use the recipient’s public key.

Keep less by default

The relay has no message database. Connections, duplicate suppression, and the open client still need temporary state. Recent frame hashes expire after 30 seconds or earlier under load. Recipients can keep copies.

Inspect the experiment

The implementation and tests are open for review. Clients use hub signalling to try direct WebRTC links. Separate Safari tabs exchanged direct dummy frames with test relay forwarding disabled, including after reconnect. Chrome direct connectivity remains unresolved; wide-area delivery and anonymity remain unproven. A single hub has no Sybil resistance and can be disrupted.

Privacy starts with honest limits.

Encryption protects content in some contexts. It does not make a connection invisible.

A hub or network observer can observe addresses, timing, message sizes, and traffic patterns. Direct peers may learn one another’s network addresses. Public messages are readable. Pseudonyms and padding do not, by themselves, prevent linking activity.

The current direct-message scheme does not provide a full forward-secrecy guarantee: compromise of a recipient’s long-term key can expose recorded messages. Signed direct messages are not deniable, and stable per-channel keys link posts over time. Browsers and the code they load are also part of the trust boundary.

“Ephemeral” describes the intended retention model. It cannot prevent screenshots, recipient archives, compromised devices, or independent infrastructure logs.

Explore the economics without real funds.

The transport should forget. A monetary system must remember enough to reject duplicate spending.

Proposed economics only. These are not purchase offers.

Ordinary Hermes credits

The proposed acquisition fee is about 5%, potentially adjusted by volume. Issuance is system-signed; transfers and IOUs are user-signed and refer to their provenance. A signature cannot create spendable value beyond what was issued.

The $100 / 47-token example is a design fixture, not a published exchange rate. The pricing and rounding rules remain to be specified. A claim to 50 tokens cannot redeem more than an issued 47.

Advertising that rewards the audience

The proposal gives advertisers more control over being seen and better confirmation of participation, while directing rewards to the person taking part. Advertising effectiveness remains to be demonstrated.

Advertising coin purchases carry a proposed 20% fee. Only separately signed, advertising-designated credits fund this rewards mechanism, which is closed only while the authority is reached through the advertiser-operated adapter. Advertisers collect and verify the required backchannel proofs against funded offers; accepted proofs support redemption within that funding limit. Ordinary credits cannot be relabeled.

Viewing evidence confirms only what the proof establishes, not human attention. Proof validation and duplicate-redemption controls still need tests against replay, fabricated evidence, bots, Sybil identities, and collusion.

Subscriptions and superlikes reward creators

Subscriptions and superlike donations are proposed ways to support creators. A pending promise is not paid revenue or a subscription entitlement. Only verified settled funds can support a payout or subscription-key release.

An unfunded or failed superlike degrades to an ordinary like. The creator does not receive an advance against it. Controlling both the sender and creator accounts creates no new value: only verified settled funds can be paid out.

What validation must establish

Separate signature domains and currency types; conservation of issued supply; duplicate-redemption rejection; expiry; and safe failure, retry, and idempotency behavior. Test that pending or failed payments never unlock keys or payouts, and that self-tipping cannot increase redeemable value.

The settlement boundary limits financial exposure from unfulfilled promises. It still needs explicit accounting and proof verification; signatures alone do not establish settled funds. Tests must also examine transaction linkability and the privacy cost of stronger evidence checks. The reference work uses dummy credits only.

python main.py
